DCSMS

Data Center Sustainability Management System.

Connecting fragmented environmental data to a clearer view of data center sustainability.

Tools

Context

DCSMS is a team project from INFO 380: Product and Information Systems Management. We designed a sustainability reporting system for Meridian Retail Group, a fictional retailer operating twelve data centers with a net-zero carbon commitment for 2040.

Working from a course scenario and mock stakeholder interviews, we developed an executive briefing, product vision, prioritized backlog, data models, and an interactive dashboard prototype. My work included product documentation, user personas, strategic priorities, a three-sprint plan, and a mock Excel dataset.

Problem

MRG’s sustainability data was scattered across facilities, collected through different tools, and reported in inconsistent formats. In the scenario, analysts spent most of their time finding and cleaning data, while compiling the annual sustainability report took about three months.

Facility managers needed a reliable way to submit measurements, analysts needed comparable data, and executives needed a clear picture of environmental performance. Compliance teams also needed to trace reported numbers back to their sources. A dashboard would only be useful if the data behind it was consistent and trustworthy.

Solution

We proposed a centralized system that collects, standardizes, validates, and displays sustainability data. Our main design decision was to prioritize data quality and visibility before more advanced reporting features.

Collect data across different facilities

The first planned sprint focused on API connections to existing building and energy management systems, with manual entry for facilities whose tools could not support automated collection. This kept older facilities within the proposed workflow.

Standardize and validate before reporting

The second planned sprint focused on consistent units, facility names, and calculations. In our workflow, valid records move into a central environmental data warehouse, while incorrect entries are flagged for correction. The data dictionary defines fields such as facility location, reporting date, energy use, water use, and carbon emissions.

Make the data useful to stakeholders

The third planned sprint brought the data into MRG’s existing Azure environment for querying and dashboards, alongside planning for auditable reporting. We used Jira to connect user stories and priorities to the sprint plan, and focused the Figma prototype on the analyst dashboard.

Product

The final deliverable is a Figma prototype of the Sustainability Analytics dashboard, supported by workflow and data flow diagrams, a data dictionary, and a prioritized product backlog.

See the overall footprint

The overview brings energy consumption, water consumption, and carbon emissions into one view. Summary cards and monthly trend charts let stakeholders review environmental performance across all data centers.

Focus on a facility

A data center selector narrows the dashboard to a specific facility. The Dublin view shows monthly totals, changes from the previous period, and daily energy trends to help analysts investigate local performance.

Inspect the underlying values

The raw data view presents dated records with explicit units alongside the visualizations. It gives analysts a way to inspect individual measurements when they need more detail than a summary chart provides.

What the prototype demonstrates

The prototype shows how standardized sustainability data could be organized and explored. Automated ingestion, the Azure pipeline, and audit-ready report generation remain proposed system capabilities. The dashboard uses sample data; improvements in reporting speed, data accuracy, and environmental performance were goals rather than measured outcomes.

So what’s next?

Our next priority would be auditability: letting someone trace a reported metric back to its source and understand how it was transformed. We left audit report generation out of the prototype so we could first focus on gathering, cleaning, and displaying the data.

The proposed roadmap progresses from data collection to transformation, visualization, and auditability. Further work would also strengthen the manual-entry workflow for facilities without API access and evaluate the design with its intended users.
